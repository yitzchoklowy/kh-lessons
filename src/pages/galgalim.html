Rambam · Hilchos Kiddush HaChodesh 14 – 16

ארבעת גלגלי הירחThe moon's four galgalim

The sun took two galgalim. The moon takes four — but not all at once. The Rambam hands them to you one chapter at a time, and each new circle exists to fix something the last picture got wrong. Start with chapter 14 and add.

the large circle יוצא מרכז — off centre גלגל קטן — the small circle the sun דומה נוטה
“The moon revolves in a small orbit that does not encompass the earth… The small orbit itself rotates in a larger orbit that encompasses the earth.” KH 14:1

Two circles, and that is the entire chapter. The large one goes round the earth and carries the small one with it. The small one never goes round the earth at all — the moon just sits on its rim.

So there are two speeds to keep, not one: how fast the small circle's centre travels round the earth, and how fast the moon travels round the small circle. Chapter 14 gives you both, and nothing else. No corrections yet — this moon is exactly where the tables say, which is not yet where anyone can see it.

you can now findאמצע הירחאמצע המסלול
“Subtract the sun's mean from the moon's mean and double the remainder.” KH 15:1

Chapter 15 tells you the same large circle twice over. First: the earth is not at its centre — it is יוצא מרכז, which gives it a near side and a far side, a גובה of its own. Second: the sun is now in the picture, because how far the moon has pulled ahead of it — doubled — changes where the moon really sits on the small circle.

And now the two lines from the earth come apart. One runs through the small circle's centre; the other runs through the moon itself. The arc between them where they meet the belt is the correction you look up, and it is the whole difference between the moon of the tables and the moon in the sky.

you can now findמרחק כפולהמסלול הנכוןמנת המסלולהירח האמיתי
“The orbit in which the moon revolves intersects the orbit in which the sun revolves at an angle, so that a portion of it is inclined to the north of the sun's orbit and a portion is inclined south.” KH 16:1

Everything so far has been flat — as though the moon travelled along the belt itself. It does not. Two more galgalim sit outside the green, both centred on the earth: the red, which lies exactly along the belt, and the blue, which leans 5° off it. They cross at two points — the ראש and the זנב.

The red drags the blue round with it, so those crossing points do not stay put: they walk backwards through the mazalos, a full circle in about eighteen and a half years. That is the last thing the calculation needs — how far the moon stands north or south of the sun's path, which decides how high it sits above the horizon when you go out to look for it.

Two motions, pulling against each other

Once all four are on the page, the thing worth holding on to is that they do not all turn the same way. The outer two carry the whole assembly one way; the green runs the other way inside them. What you see is only what is left.

גלגל דומהdomeh

Domeh — it resembles the belt of mazalos, lying exactly under it with the earth at its centre. It is the frame the other three are measured against, and it drags the blue round with it.

Which step comes from which

Every circle in the drawing is doing a job in the arithmetic.

GalgalWhat it producesWhere
קטן The maslul — where the moon sits on the rim — and from it the correction that turns אמצע into אמיתי. KH 14:3, 15:4–6
יוצא מרכז Its גובה, and — with the sun pulling the small circle round it — the double distance and the corrected course the table is read with. KH 15:1–3
דומה The frame everything is measured in, and, by dragging the blue, the backwards march of the ראש through the mazalos. KH 16:2–4
נוטה The moon's latitude — how far north or south of the sun's path it sits, up to 5°. The last thing the visibility test asks for. KH 16:9–10
