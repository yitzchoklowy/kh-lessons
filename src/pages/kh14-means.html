The small circle is at its true proportion — radius 5° of the big one — and the line runs through the moon.

Both circles turn evenly. The angle from the earth does not: 12° 7′ 51″ to 14° 25′ 13″ in a day, never more than 5° from the mean. not his — geometry of these two circles; his table is KH 15:6, which parts from it by up to 1° 23′.

Both circles turn perfectly evenly. What is not even is the angle you see them at from the earth: near the far point of the small circle the moon's own travel adds to the carry, and half a turn later it works against it. Over four hundred days this geometry runs between 12° 7′ 51″ and 14° 25′ 13″ in a day, and the sight line never leaves the mean by more than 5° — exactly the small circle's radius, and the size of the correction table the Rambam hands you in chapter 15. He does not compute it here; the picture is only showing why the next chapter needs a table at all. (Chapter 15's answer also folds in the sun's pull and the off-centre, so it parts company with this bare geometry by up to 1° 23′.)

Next: KH 14:5–6, where the sun nudges this mean →

KH 14:1“There are two mean rates of progress with regard to the moon, for the moon revolves in a small orbit that does not encompass the earth… The small orbit itself rotates in a larger orbit that encompasses the earth.”
KH 14:3“The distance travelled by the mean within its path in a single day is 13 degrees, 3 minutes and 54 seconds” — and reckoned east to west, against the movement of the sphere. That is why the moon runs round the small circle the opposite way to the way the big circle carries it.
KH 14:4“The position of the moon's mean on Wednesday night, the third of Nisan 4938… was 1° 14′ 43″ in the constellation of Taurus. The mean within its path at this date was 84° 28′ 42″.”

Step two — now the sun walks in

Step one was reckoned for six o'clock. But the chapter wants the moon's mean at sunset, and sunset moves through the year. So the Rambam asks one question — where is the sun? — and answers it with a nudge of at most half a degree.

KH 14:5“If the sun is located between midway in the constellation of Pisces and midway in the constellation of Aries, the moon's mean should be left without emendation. If the sun is located between midway in Aries and the beginning of Gemini, 15 minutes should be added…”
KH 14:6“The figure that remains after these additions or subtractions have been made, or when the mean was left without emendation, is the mean of the moon approximately 20 minutes after the setting of the sun… This is referred to as the mean of the moon at the time of the sighting.”
The sun's own position is worked out in chapters 12–13, which is why it could not appear in step one. Everything else on this page is chapter 14's own.
